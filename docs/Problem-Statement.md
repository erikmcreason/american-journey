# The American Journey Problem Statement

## Problem

Immigrants often struggle throughout the immigration and integration process because the path is confusing, fragmented, expensive, and difficult to navigate. They may face challenges with preparation, paperwork, deadlines, English, financial literacy, cultural understanding, employment readiness, citizenship preparation, and knowing which steps matter most.

Many immigrants want to succeed, contribute, and become part of American society, but they lack a clear roadmap that helps them understand what to do, when to do it, why it matters, and what risks they face if they miss important steps.

## Who Experiences This Problem

This problem affects many immigrants, including legal immigrants, undocumented immigrants, future immigrants, green card applicants, permanent residents, refugees, asylum seekers, international students, and families supporting someone through the immigration process.

The problem is often even more difficult for people with fewer resources, less education, limited English, limited money, or little understanding of American systems.

## Cost of the Problem

The cost can be severe. Immigrants may miss deadlines, misunderstand paperwork, lose money to predatory lawyers or fake legal services, delay their immigration process, lose opportunities, struggle financially, remain socially isolated, fail to prepare for citizenship, or even face deportation.

Poor integration can also create long-term hardship, including poverty, weak career opportunities, lack of confidence, and difficulty becoming fully engaged members of American society.

## Desired Outcome

The American Journey creates a structured pathway that helps immigrants prepare for, navigate, and succeed in the immigration and integration process.

The platform helps users build English skills, understand financial systems, prepare for citizenship, manage administrative requirements, avoid scams, integrate into American culture, and preserve the valuable parts of their own heritage.

Success means immigrants have a clearer path, stronger support, better preparation, and a greater ability to thrive as productive contributors, future citizens, and culturally rooted Americans.
